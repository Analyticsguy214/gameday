AP voters: how they behave

Sixty-plus writers fill out ballots on Saturday night. They start from last week's ballot and change as little as they can. The model copies that habit.

  1. Inertia first. Every team starts from its current AP points. base
  2. Win and hold. A win over an unranked team barely moves you. You rise mainly when teams above you lose. +6 to +25
  3. Ranked wins pay. Beating a ranked team earns more the higher that team is ranked. Blowouts add style points. +55 to +225
  4. Losses are punished by who you lost to. Losing to an unranked team costs about a third of your points. Losing to a ranked team costs far less, and less again if it was close. −16% to −32%
  5. Byes slip. Idle teams get passed by teams that won. −12
  6. Unbeaten bias. Voters protect undefeated Power Four teams. +25
  7. Slow resume drift. Over time ballots move toward what the resume says, so each week 12% of the gap to the committee model closes. 12%

CFP committee: what it measures

The 13-member committee re-ranks from scratch each Tuesday and says it does not look at the polls. It reads resumes. The model scores every FBS team on the same inputs.

  1. Strength of record. Wins compared with what a Top-25 team would expect against that schedule. 40%
  2. Power rating. Margin of victory (capped at 24) adjusted for opponent and home field. Stands in for "game control" and the eye test. 25%
  3. Quality wins. Wins over current Top-25 teams. 12%
  4. Strength of schedule. Average rating of every opponent played. 10%
  5. Perception. The committee says it ignores polls, but its rankings track them, so a small share of AP points is included. 13%
  6. Bad losses. Each loss to a team outside the top 60 is a heavy penalty. Group of Five teams carry a schedule discount. penalty
  7. Head-to-head. When two teams sit within four spots and one beat the other with no worse record, the winner goes ahead. tiebreak
